# Coding Guidelines

Each row contains one participant message.

Code **only `user_message_to_code`**.

You may use `previous_agent_response_context` to understand what the participant is referring to, especially in short messages such as “why?”, “no”, or “what happened next?”, but **do not code the character’s response itself**.

`source_row_index` is a technical identifier only and must not be used when making coding decisions.

Do not attempt to infer or guess the participant’s country, interface type, character, or any other information about the participant. Base your judgment only on the conversation text shown.

Select **one label only** for each field.

## 1. Tone

* `neutral` — matter-of-fact or direct, no prominent tone.
* `casual` — very conversational, abbreviated, or slang-like.
* `respectful` — clear respect, appreciation, or prominent politeness, explicitly expressed toward the character.
* `emotional` — the participant explicitly expresses their own emotion (sadness, fear, shock, joy, excitement, or similar).
* `uncertain` — the participant explicitly expresses hesitation, doubt, or confusion.
* `confrontational` — direct challenge, accusation, disagreement, or criticism.

A difficult or traumatic topic alone is not sufficient to label a message as `emotional`.

Spelling or grammatical errors alone are not sufficient to label a message as `casual`.


## 2. Address Form

* `informal_you` — ordinary second-person address, for example `you/your`.
* `formal_you` — highly formal address or an explicitly formal second-person form. In languages with a grammatical distinction, such as `Sie` in German, use this category.
* `named_honorific` — address by name or title, for example Ben, Mr. Lesser, Sir.
* `no_direct_address` — no direct form of address.
* `other` — another form of address.

In English, ordinary `you` should generally be coded as `informal_you`.

Do not code `formal_you` merely because the wording is polite.

## 3. Question Style

* `experiential` — asks the character to describe a personal experience, feeling, or something they personally went through.
* `factual_open` — an open-ended request for factual information: who, what, where, when, or how, without emphasizing personal experience or a process.
* `factual_closed` — asks for a specific or constrained answer, usually yes/no or a short factual detail.
* `evaluative` — asks for an opinion, judgment, meaning, importance, or lesson.
* `relational` — an interpersonal act such as a greeting, thanks, apology, concern, or empathy.
* `procedural` — asks about a process, sequence, or progression of events: how something happened or what happened next.
* `other` — does not clearly fit the other categories.

Decision rules:

* Personal experience or how the person experienced something → `experiential`
* Process / sequence / what happened next → `procedural`
* Open factual information → `factual_open`
* Yes/no answer or a specific detail → `factual_closed`

The word `how` alone does not determine the category. Consider what the participant is actually asking to know.

## Relational vs. Emotional

If the main function of the message is a social or interpersonal act, for example:

“Thank you”
“I’m sorry”
“I’m with you”

→ `relational`

If emotion is a central part of the question or message itself, rather than merely an interpersonal gesture, `emotional` may be selected for Tone.

Important: Tone and Question Style are separate fields, so a message may, for example, be `emotional` in Tone and `relational` in Question Style.

## In Cases of Uncertainty

If the decision is clear:

`human_uncertain = no`

If two categories both seem plausible and the distinction is difficult:

1. Choose the category that fits best.
2. Mark `human_uncertain = yes`.
3. In `human_notes`, briefly state which categories you were deciding between.
